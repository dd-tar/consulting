How I work

Make the uncertainty explicit.

Good research does not manufacture conviction. It reduces the decision to what is supported, what is inferred and what still needs to be tested.

01

Frame the decision

What must be decided, by whom, by when — and what changes depending on the answer?

02

Collect the missing evidence

Interviews, documents, market research, architecture review and the smallest useful experiment.

03

Produce a usable decision

A short owned document: recommendation, alternatives, scope, requirements, risks and next actions.

Known

Observed or supported by a source.

Inferred

A conclusion with its reasoning visible.

Unknown

Unresolved, with the next useful test named.

Questions

Before we speak.

What does a first engagement look like?

A short fit call, then — if there is a bounded question worth answering — a fixed-scope Decision & Discovery Sprint lasting one to two weeks. It ends with a usable document, not a proposal for indefinite consulting.

We already have developers or an agency. Where do you fit?

Before implementation or alongside its earliest stage: clarifying the problem, interviewing stakeholders, comparing options and producing requirements. I can work directly with your delivery team or white-label inside an agency engagement.

Can you help us decide where AI is useful?

Yes. AI is treated as one possible solution, not the starting assumption. I can identify suitable workflows, define the cheapest useful pilot and its success criteria — or recommend a simpler system when it would work better.

What if the evidence does not support our preferred answer?

The conclusion will say so. I distinguish observed facts, supported inferences and unresolved unknowns, then name what evidence or experiment would change the decision.

Do you write code?

I have a software-engineering background, can read code and test technical claims, and build my own products. I use that depth to improve discovery and assessment work; I do not take staff-augmentation engineering roles.
